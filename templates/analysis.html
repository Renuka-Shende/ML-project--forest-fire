Increasing Forest Fire Alerts: A Worrying Trend

Recent years have seen a worrying trend in the frequency and severity of forest fires worldwide. From the Amazon rainforest to the Siberian taiga and the forests of California, wildfires have become more prevalent, posing significant threats to ecosystems, wildlife, and human communities. Over the last 20 years, fire-related tree cover loss in the tropics increased at a rate of about 36,000 hectares (around 5%) per year and accounted for roughly 15% of the total global increase in tree cover loss from fires between 2001 and 2024.



About Our Largest Rainforest: The Amazon Rainforest

The Amazon rainforest stands as the world's largest rainforest, playing a crucial role by providing approximately 30% of the Earth's oxygen, effectively serving as the planet's lungs. As part of our research efforts, we have conducted data analysis focusing on forest fire incidents specifically within the Brazilian region of the Amazon rainforest.

About the dataset:

This dataset report of the number of forest fires in Brazil divided by states. The series comprises the period of approximately 10 years (1998 to 2017). The data is obtained from the official website of the Brazilian government.

  • This graph shows the yearly trend of forest fires from 1998 to 2017.
  • It can be observed that forest fires were at their peak in the years 2003 and 2016.
  • The primary observation from the graph indicates a gradual increase in the frequency of forest fires between 1998 and 2017. Specifically, there has been a rise from 20,000 incidents to 36,000 which represent a significant 80% surge.

  • This graph shows the monthly trend of forest fires from 1998 to 2017.
  • It can be observed that forest fires were at their peak in the months from July to November.
  • These months fall within Brazil's dry season, where the climate becomes drier due to reduced rainfall.
  • The combination of high temperatures, low humidity, and dry vegetation increases the risk of forest fires during this period.

Here are some facts about losses resulting from forest fires:

2021 was one of the worst years for forest fires since the turn of the century, causing an alarming 9.3 million hectares of tree cover loss globally, over one-third of all tree cover loss that occurred that year.

From 2001 to 2022, India lost 3.59 lakh hectares of tree cover from fires and 2.15 million hectares from all other drivers of loss. And about 5.2 million hectares globally.

The Annual 2022 Wildfires Report from the National Centers for Environmental Information indicates that over 7.5 million acres of wildland were consumed by fire that year.

Nearly 17 million vertebrate creatures, animals like snakes, small birds and rodents are estimated to have been killed throughout the wildfires in the Pantanal region of Brazil in 2020.
